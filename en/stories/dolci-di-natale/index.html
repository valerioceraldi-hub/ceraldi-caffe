Christmas sweets in Naples: struffoli, roccocò and mostaccioli

Honey, almonds, pisto and colourful diavulilli. In Naples, Christmas is recognised first of all by its sweets.

By the Ceraldi family. Published 2026-10-18. 4 min read. Pastries

Christmas in Naples has a precise scent: warm honey, toasted almonds, orange zest and pisto, the spice blend (cinnamon, cloves, nutmeg and others) that goes into almost every festive sweet.

Struffoli

Small balls of fried dough, bound with honey and decorated with candied fruit and coloured sprinkles, the diavulilli. Struffoli are made in large quantities, given as gifts and stay on the table for days. They resemble sweets from other Mediterranean traditions, such as Greek loukoumades.

Roccocò

Hard, crunchy rings with almonds, orange zest and pisto. Tradition traces them back to the fourteenth century and to the nuns of the Real Convento della Maddalena. They are hard by nature: many people soften them in wine, such as marsala.

Mostaccioli

Soft honey and spice biscuits covered in chocolate. Originally the dough contained grape must, which gave them their name.

Susamielli, raffioli and divino amore

  • Susamielli: S-shaped biscuits with honey, almonds and pisto, covered in sesame seeds.
  • Raffioli: discs of sponge cake with apricot jam and white icing.
  • Divino amore: almond paste with candied fruit and jam, under a pink icing.

Christmas in the bars

During the holidays Christmas sweets also arrive in the bars, next to the coffee. And the pastiera, the Easter sweet par excellence, is increasingly found in Naples at Christmas too.
